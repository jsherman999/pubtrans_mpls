Activity

    Minneapolis network lab

    Real streets, illustrative operations. Vehicles follow a directed graph derived from OpenStreetMap road centerlines through downtown Minneapolis and both University of Minnesota campus banks.

    Explore

    The entire area is fitted on startup. Drag to pan; use the wheel, pinch, or +/− buttons to zoom. Saved views jump to each district. Click a vehicle for its live state, then follow it if desired.

    Queue monitor

    The graph beneath the map tracks queued requests over the last 30 simulated minutes. Large digits show the current queue; the dashed amber threshold and run-peak digits show the maximum queue reached since the last reset. All requests are generated automatically and dispatched using directed Dijkstra routing.

    Fleet and transit hub

    Sedan, shuttle, and bus counts adjust independently, up to 60 of each type. Empty vehicles without a dispatch return to the Central Transit Hub at Nicollet & 7th and wait until a compatible passenger request is available.

    Building-based demand

    Requests originate across 933 real named buildings, including apartments, townhomes, shops, employers, and campus dorms. Demand can reach 600 requests per hour. Large dorms such as Frontier Hall are weighted for dozens of hourly calls, with residential, employment, campus, and retail patterns changing by time of day.

    Signals and human drivers

    Directed streets constrain every route. Timed signals create red-light stops at selected intersections. Human cars enter from the area boundary, drive to a shop or workplace, dwell for an errand, and exit. Their entry rate adjusts from 20 to 200 cars per hour. Fleet vehicles detect human traffic up to 70 meters ahead and slow or stop to avoid it.

    Accuracy

    Street and building geometry is data-derived. Local one-way defaults, speeds, demand, signal placement/timing, human traffic, and operations are illustrative. The Mississippi polygon is approximate. This is not a calibrated municipal traffic model.

    Everything runs in your browser from the checked-in dataset. No account, API key, tile service, or backend is used.
